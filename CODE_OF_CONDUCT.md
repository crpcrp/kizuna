# Code of Conduct

This applies to everyone taking part in this project — issues, pull requests,
discussions, code review, and commit messages alike. It applies to the
maintainer as much as to anyone else.

## What is expected

- Address the work, not the person. "This deinflection table misses the causative
  form" is review; "you clearly don't know Japanese" is not.
- Assume the other person is trying. Contributors here have wildly different
  levels of Japanese, of TypeScript, and of Electron, and none of those is a
  qualification to participate.
- Accept that a decision may go against you and say so plainly rather than
  relitigating it in every thread.
- Keep other people's data out of it. Do not post someone's personal
  information, and do not paste tokens, passwords, or private files — yours or
  anyone else's.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, intimidation, or sustained hostility, in public or in private
  messages that begin here.
- Insults or dismissals aimed at someone's identity — nationality, race,
  ethnicity, sex, gender identity or expression, sexual orientation, disability,
  age, religion, or level of experience. Japanese learners and native speakers
  are equally welcome, and neither gets to talk down to the other.
- Sexual language or imagery, and unwelcome sexual or romantic attention.
- Publishing someone's private information without their explicit permission.
- Deliberately derailing threads, or repeatedly reopening a settled decision to
  wear people down.

## Reporting

Report a problem privately to the maintainer, [@crpcrp](https://github.com/crpcrp),
through GitHub. Do not open a public issue about someone's conduct — it makes
the situation worse for the person reporting it. If the report concerns the
maintainer, or you would rather not contact them, use GitHub's own
[report abuse](https://github.com/contact/report-abuse) form.

Reports are handled privately. Whoever reports something in good faith will not
be penalised for it, whatever the outcome.

## Consequences

The maintainer decides what happens, in proportion to what occurred. In
escalating order: a private word, a public correction, edited or deleted
comments, a temporary block, and a permanent ban from the repository. Serious
cases — threats, harassment campaigns, doxxing — skip straight to the end.

Enforcement is not a debate. If you disagree with an outcome, say so once,
privately.
